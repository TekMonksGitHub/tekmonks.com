The Landscape Has Changed

The two pillars enterprises were leaning on have cracked.

Organisations that built AI strategies around open-source models or Chinese AI providers are now facing a governance crisis. The ground has shifted — and it shifted fast.

⚠️
Risk Category 1

Open Source AI:
The Gates Are Closing

For years, Meta's Llama series was the bedrock of "sovereign AI" strategies worldwide. That era is ending. Meta has launched Muse Spark — its first frontier model released as a closed, proprietary system. The open-source roadmap is now explicitly uncertain.

  • Llama 4 was not released as a true open-source model — the OSI formally ruled its licence does not meet the Open Source Definition, and the FSF classified Llama 3.1 as a non-free licence.
  • Meta's new Muse Spark model launched exclusively as proprietary — the first Meta model without open weights — signalling a fundamental strategic reversal.
  • Meta's Chief AI Officer confirmed the pivot: the company rebuilt its AI stack from scratch with a closed architecture, spending $72–115 billion on proprietary infrastructure.
  • Whether the Llama series will continue to be developed at all was left explicitly open by Meta when pressed by the developer community.
  • Enterprises that built products on Llama now face the same dependency risk they believed open-source would protect them from.
🚫
Risk Category 2

Chinese AI Models:
A Sovereign Red Line

DeepSeek and Qwen are technically impressive. But for any government, regulated enterprise, or organisation handling sensitive data, they represent an unacceptable and unmanageable risk under current geopolitical and legal conditions.

  • Under China's 2017 National Intelligence Law, every Chinese AI company must "support, assist, and cooperate with state intelligence work" — with no legal mechanism to refuse.
  • Every API call to a Chinese-hosted model transmits your query, context, documents, and conversation history to servers subject to Chinese state access. There is no legal remedy comparable to a Western court subpoena.
  • DeepSeek's own apps were found to transmit device identifiers, keystroke patterns, and IP addresses unencrypted. Australia, Italy, Taiwan, South Korea, and the US have banned the app on government devices.
  • The AI ecosystem is now bifurcating along geopolitical lines. Enterprises evaluating Chinese AI providers face escalating supply chain risk, data sovereignty exposure, and long-term vendor instability.
  • India, France, and South Korea have specifically flagged DeepSeek for privacy and security non-compliance. For Indian government clients, the choice is not ambiguous.
